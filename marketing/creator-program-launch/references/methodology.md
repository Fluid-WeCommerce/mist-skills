# Running a creator program on Fluid

For consumer brands that want customers and small creators selling for them, without adding an affiliate app.

## The short version

The usual setup is a store, an affiliate app, a sheet of coupon codes and a payout tool, with someone reconciling them every month. The reconciling exists because the store does not know who sent the order.

On Fluid the store knows. Each creator shares the real store under their own link, and the order carries their name from the link through the cart to the receipt, as long as the shopper stays in the same browser. So a creator program comes down to four jobs: decide the plan, give creators their link, keep a ledger, pay on schedule.

## Is the brand ready

Check these before recruiting anyone.

- The product earns a reorder or a recommendation on its own. Creators speed up word of mouth that already exists.
- There is margin to pay a commission after product cost, shipping, payment fees and whatever discount followers get.
- The product page converts ordinary traffic. Sending creators' followers to a weak page wastes the creator's goodwill.
- Someone owns the program. A named person answers creators, ships samples and runs the payout.
- The brand has published its own content first. Creators copy the tone they see.

## The seven decisions

Make these once, write them on one page, and give that page to every creator.

1. **What to call them.** Creators, ambassadors, partners. Pick one word and use it everywhere.
2. **The rate.** Work it out from your own margin. Example only: on a $60 order with $33 left after product, shipping and fees, a 15% commission is $9 and a 10% follower discount is $6, which leaves $18. If that number does not work for you, change the rate or the discount before launch, not after.
3. **One level or two.** Start with one: a creator earns on the orders they send. Add a second when creators are already bringing in other creators and you want to thank them for it. The rules for the second level are below.
4. **Which orders earn.** Every order, a customer's first order only, or the first order plus subscription renewals for a set time. Subscription brands should decide this on purpose, because renewals are where the cost adds up.
5. **The hold.** A commission waits out your return window before it can be paid. If the order is refunded, the commission is reversed.
6. **The payout.** A fixed day, a minimum balance and a method. Monthly is easiest to keep.
7. **Who gets in.** Invite only, or open applications that you approve.

## Rules for a second level

A second level means a creator who brings in another creator earns a smaller share of that creator's sales to customers. Keep it inside these lines.

- It is always a share of product sold to real customers. Nobody is paid for signing someone up.
- Joining is free. There is no kit to buy and no purchase required to earn.
- The second-level rate is lower than the first.
- Two levels is the limit. A plan with more levels is a different kind of business with different rules.

Have counsel read the plan before it is public. This page is a method, and no lawyer has reviewed your version of it.

## How it runs on Fluid

| Job | On Fluid | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A creator account and link | A member type with "Can act as rep" on, and a username. Their own page is `/my/<username>` on your store, and anything they share from the app carries the same credit | Built in |
| Credit for the order | Fair Share carries credit from the link through the cart to the order, and you set the rules: first touch, last touch or most activity when two creators reach one shopper | Built in |
| Tools for the creator | Your branded app with a Share tab, share links, smart links to one contact, and a personal MySite page | Built in |
| Seeing who is winning | Traffic > Reps ranks creators by visitors sent, with orders, revenue and top asset | Built in |
| A perk for followers | Promo codes with limits on total uses, one use per customer, minimum spend, country and dates | Built in |
| The commission ledger | An app built in Mist to your plan, one or two levels, with refunds reversed and a statement at period close. You approve it before it goes live | Built with Mist |
| Paying creators | A statement you pay from through your own bank or payout tool, or store credit where reward points are on | You pay; the ledger keeps score |
| More countries | The Country Atlas and the Open a Country flow: currency, payment methods, language, agreements | Built in |
| Social channels | Creators post their link in bios, posts and messages. Selling inside a marketplace or a shop tab is a separate connection; ask your Fluid team what is available for your account | Links built in; marketplaces by account |

## Order of work

1. Approve the plan.
2. Set up the store side: creator type, credit rules, sign-up path, terms, share kit.
3. Build the ledger and prove it with one test order, including a refund.
4. Invite the first group. Start with customers who already buy and already post.
5. Send product. A sample is a gift, and a post has to be earned.
6. Go live and start the weekly rhythm.

## The weekly rhythm

- **Look at the leaderboard.** Visitors, orders and revenue by creator, and which asset did the work.
- **Feed the winners.** More product, early access to the next drop, a higher rate for the few who have earned it.
- **Refresh what they share.** Retire tired assets and add new ones so nobody runs out of things to post.
- **Read the ledger.** What is pending, what is payable, what was reversed and why.
- **Once a month, pay.** On the day you said you would.

## Best practices

- **Recruit from your customers first.** They already know the product and their recommendation is true.
- **Choose on fit and selling evidence.** A small audience that buys beats a large one that scrolls. Follower count on its own tells you little.
- **Let the link carry the credit.** A code can be passed around. A link in the creator's name is harder to borrow. Keep the code as a perk for followers.
- **Start with one rate.** Raise it for proven creators one at a time. Tiers nobody can explain do not motivate anyone.
- **Pay on product, after discounts.** Not on tax or shipping.
- **Give creators a discount on their own orders** in place of a commission on them.
- **Hold for the return window and reverse on refund,** and say so before a creator joins.
- **Pay on time, every time.** A late payout costs more trust than a low rate.
- **Put disclosure in the terms and the guide.** Regulators expect a creator to say plainly when they earn from a brand; in the US, read the FTC's Endorsement Guides. Your counsel confirms the wording for each country you sell in.
- **Give creators the claims they may make.** For anything touching health, hand them approved wording and review what goes out.
- **Get content rights in writing** if you want to reuse a creator's posts in your own ads.
- **Collect tax details before the first payout.** Your accountant sets what you report and when.
- **Remember that commission is one lever.** Product access, early drops and working with the brand keep good creators longer than a rate change.
- **Measure against a baseline.** Know what the store did before the program, so you can tell new sales from sales you would have had anyway.

## What not to promise a creator

- An income. Say what the rate is and let the orders speak.
- A payout method you have not set up yet.
- Credit for orders that did not come through their link or their name.
