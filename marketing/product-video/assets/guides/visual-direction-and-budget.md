# Brand-specific direction with a bounded polish pass

Use for a new film or when a film feels generic. For a focused edit, retain its approved direction and review only the changed shots and adjoining seams.

## Decide from the company, not the category

From already discovered source material, note three to five distinctive, verified things: a recognizable object/ingredient, visual asset, actual interface action, company constraint or brand ritual. Do not add research just to fill a quota. Select one visual idea grounded in those specifics and the user's chosen references. Ask whether it could advertise a competitor unchanged; if so, make the subject/action more specific.

Write the idea and its proof in the existing brief/story. For each beat use **point → visible demonstration → payoff**. A page title and a device mockup are not the demonstration. Reuse a requested signature such as the layered flyover, and let supporting moves clarify real actions: an ingredient/photo swap, an attached preview lifting into a phone, or a map's populated pins. Do not invent product features or data to satisfy an effect recipe.

Prefer the company's existing assets and approved motion kit. One well-developed signature plus a few supporting techniques is usually sufficient. Keep brand colors, source typography/copy, actual UI cards/dividers, and coherent physical shadows. Taste Skill's website-specific bans, image quotas, random direction draw and responsive page-building workflow do not apply automatically to a source-faithful product video. Do not redesign the underlying site merely to style the film.

## Look before spending on the full export

Review the focal still of each new/changed scene, then a compact sequence of its defining motion. For a fragile handoff, capture four closely spaced times across the overlap, not only a settled frame before and after. Inspect depth order, doubled imagery/text, perspective alignment, clipping/shadows, focus and reading time. Include the full-page camera view when a flyover uses it. Use the existing low/normal-resolution frame tool; reserve high resolution for small type, edge or material concerns.

Make one concise critique of the actual pictures, ranked by viewer impact. Fix up to the three most important observed issues in one pass, then recheck only the affected motion/boundaries. Do not create a jury of agents, mandatory image-generation variants, exhaustive website audits or repeated style redraws for an ordinary run. Broader exploration is a separate user-selected scope.

A targeted visual/functional failure still needs repair and verification. If the bounded pass reveals a larger art-direction problem, preserve the reviewable version and explain the specific remaining work instead of silently starting an open-ended redesign loop. Never call an unchecked or visibly broken result finished because a pass budget is used up.

## Default effort and cost

- Keep discovery and the coverage quiz short; derive production defaults from the existing brief.
- Reuse approved captures, local media, models, music and deterministic animation helpers; regenerate only what the change needs.
- Default to standard Three.js at the chosen delivery size/FPS. Existing material/lighting work is still important in this mode.
- Prefer representative frame/motion checks and one full standard export. Repeat a full export only for a concrete correction that affects the deliverable, not to reroll taste or chase tiny numerical scores.
- Image-generation campaigns, paid cloud rendering and a full path-traced finish are optional, not hidden prerequisites. Offer the relevant upgrade and its measured or clearly stated estimated resource cost before starting it.
- Do not promise a fixed dollar cost or token ceiling without actual metering. These are bounds on the workflow's effort, not a billing guarantee.

The native quiz still asks what the film should cover, with discovered choices and a typed answer. This reference adds no mandatory technical questionnaire.
